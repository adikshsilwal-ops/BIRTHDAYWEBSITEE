IDK IF YOU WILL READ THIS OR NOT

EARRINGS, COFFEE AND 45 MINUTES OF HEAVEN

It was 1st of february, a freezing, pitchblack morning, but my chest was buzzing with this crazy, electric excitement. It was the day of the World Scholar's Cup regional round.

Back then, I was still navigating the quiet wreck of a recent breakup. even though we had called it quits, we were trapped in this sweet, painful loop of talking every single day and night. a part of me was still desperately clinging to the hope that maybe, just maybe, we’d find our way back to each other.

That morning, we were supposed to be at school at 6:00 AM sharp to make the drive to premier school by 8:30. I remember pulling myself out of bed at 5, getting ready in the dark, and finishing by 5:30. But instead of leaving, I deliberately texted her saying I’d overslept and completely forgot about WSC a stupid, selfish little lie just so I could steal a few extra minutes in the warmth of talking with her.

Then I got to school. And that’s when everything changed.

Right there, standing in the morning chill, I saw her the most breathtaking girl I had ever laid eyes on. I still remember the way she looked so vividly. She was wearing these simple earrings a friend had gifted her, and in that single, fleeting moment, she left an indelible mark on my heart that I knew would never fade.

My teammates arrived, and soon enough, the school bus loaded up and headed out towards premier school. Out of 40 students split between two buses, fate put me and her on the exact same bus.

We finally pulled up to Premier and stepped out into the morning air, waiting around for a bit before going inside. I put my headphones on, letting the music drown out the noise around me, just watching her...

((I LOVE YOU SMARIKAAAAAA, I AM SO SO SORRY FOR ALL THE THINGS I TOLD YOU AND EVERYTHING I DID. I MISS YOU AND IT FREAKING HURTS)). (just me casually having a full breakdown mid-sentence).

Anyway... continuing from the headphones part, we reached Premier. But the second I stepped off the bus, I felt the ghostly presence of my ex hanging over me. Surprisingly though, it didn't even bother me that much anymore.

Then came the moment that changed everything. Rajbin walks up to me out of nowhere and asks if I wanted to patch up with her. (OMG I WAS LITERALLY OVER THE MOON). Without a single second thought, I said yes in that moment, I genuinely thought everything was falling back into place.

A bit later, Kamal sir came around to distribute our WSC ID cards. Once we got them, everyone was told to head over to the main hall for the opening ceremony. But me and my friends decided to grab some coffee first before heading inside.

By the time we walked into the hall with our coffees, we were dead last. The entire back was completely packed, so we were forced to go all the way down and sit right in the very front. Fate worked its magic, and Smarika and I ended up sitting side by side.

And then... WE HELD HANDS FOR 45 MINUTES STRAIGHT.

If anyone ever asks me for my final wish on this earth, I wouldn't ask for time or riches. I’d tell them I just want to pause the entire universe right there and trap myself in that exact moment to replay those 45 minutes on an endless loop forever, holding her hand while the rest of the world fades away.

A freezing, pitch-black morning,
my chest buzzing with electric heat.
Then the darkness cleared,
and there she was.
Standing in the chill,
simple earrings framing her face,
a gift from a friend,
making the cold world completely disappear.
No noise, no ghosts, no past left to fight
just breathtaking, effortless grace
holding the room without even trying.

When we got back, it was time for the Scholar's Challenge and Collaborative Writing. Even though our teams were separate, her team and my team were in the same area, and we chose to sit right next to each other again.

While we were sitting there, I noticed she was shivering from the cold. I had this sudden flash of hesitation, we were at an official school event, representing our school, and we both wanted to keep things on the low. If anyone from school caught her wearing my coat, it’d definitely raise eyebrows and spark rumors. But watching her sit there freezing, none of that warning noise in my head mattered anymore. I couldn't bear to see her cold, so I took off my coat and gave it to her anyway.

A moment later, my friend looked over and asked in Nepali, "Yo coat kasko ho?"

She looked right back and replied, "Mero."

Bro, just that one single word, claiming it as hers, completely melted my heartfelt right then and there.

And then we finished the event and returned back to school and from there we returned to our home.

Some moments are short. Some moments stay.
